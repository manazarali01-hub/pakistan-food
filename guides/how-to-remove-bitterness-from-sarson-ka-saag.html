How to Balance Bitterness in Sarson ka Saag

Quick answer: Mustard greens naturally have a pleasant bitterness; balance it with other greens, proper cooking, salt and a rich finishing tarka rather than removing every bitter note.

Some bitterness is part of mustard greens

Sarson has a naturally sharper flavour than spinach, so the goal is usually balance rather than removing every bitter note. A completely neutral result can lose the character that distinguishes mustard-green saag.

Use a blend of greens when appropriate

Spinach, bathua or other greens used in regional and family recipes can soften mustard's assertive edge. The proportion determines whether sarson remains dominant or becomes much milder.

Cook until the raw harshness mellows

Fresh mustard greens can taste sharp when undercooked. Patient cooking softens the leaves and develops a rounder flavour. Stir and monitor moisture so the greens cook without scorching.

Use cornmeal for body, not to hide flavour

Makki ka atta is often used in small amounts to help thicken and bind the saag. Add it according to the recipe and cook it properly so there is no raw flour taste.

Finish with a flavourful tarka

Ghee, garlic and chilli provide richness and aroma that balance the greens. Salt also changes how bitterness is perceived, so make final seasoning adjustments only after the saag has cooked down.

Avoid correcting with lots of sugar

A tiny sweet note may appear in some household methods, but heavy sugar can mask the savoury character. First correct undercooking, seasoning and the balance of greens before reaching for sweetness.
