How to Bloom Spices in Oil Without Burning Them

Quick answer: Warm oil or ghee over medium-low heat. Add whole spices first, then ground spices for only a few seconds; lower the heat or add the next moist ingredient before the spices darken.

Choose the right fat and pan

Use a heavy-bottomed pan and enough oil or ghee to coat the spices. A thin pan can develop hot spots, so lower the flame if the cumin or other small spices darken before they smell fragrant.

Heat the fat without smoking it

Heat the fat until it shimmers gently, not until it smokes. If it starts smoking, take the pan off the burner briefly and let it cool before adding spices; smoking fat can scorch spices quickly.

Add whole spices before delicate ground spices

Add whole spices such as cumin, bay leaf, cinnamon, cardamom or cloves first. Stir for roughly 20–40 seconds, adjusting to the spice size; stop when the aroma rises, not when the spices turn dark brown.

Watch for aroma, sizzling and colour

Ground chilli, turmeric and coriander powder burn faster than whole spices. Reduce the heat or briefly move the pan off the burner, stir the powders into the fat for only a few seconds, then add tomatoes, yogurt or a small splash of water as the recipe requires.

Prevent ground spices from scorching

A fragrant smell and gentle sizzling are useful cues. Acrid smoke, blackened powder or a bitter burnt smell means the spices have scorched; adding more masala usually will not remove that bitterness, so restarting the spice base is safer.

Move to the next ingredient at the right moment

If the masala looks dry before the onions or tomatoes are ready, lower the heat and add liquid a teaspoon or tablespoon at a time rather than turning the flame up. Keep stirring so ground spices do not stick to the bottom.

Roman Urdu: Masalay oil mein kaise bloom karein?

Roman Urdu: Masalay ko halki darmiyani aanch par oil ya ghee mein pakayein. Sabit masalay pehle daalein, phir pisa hua masala chand seconds ke liye; jalne ki tez ya karwi boo aaye to masala scorch ho chuka hai.
