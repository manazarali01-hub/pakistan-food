Lahori Chargha vs Sajji: What Is the Difference?

Quick answer: Chargha is typically deeply seasoned and cooked for a richly spiced exterior, while sajji is associated with simpler seasoning and a roast-style preparation; recipes vary by region and cook.

Seasoning style

Chargha commonly uses a more assertive spice marinade. Sajji often puts more emphasis on the flavour of the meat, salt and roasting, though modern versions vary widely.

Cooking approach

Both can involve whole or large pieces of chicken, but home methods differ: steaming plus frying or roasting may be used for chargha, while sajji is strongly associated with roasting.

Which should you cook?

Choose chargha when you want a bold spiced coating; choose sajji when you want a simpler roast-style profile. Follow the individual recipe rather than treating the names as interchangeable.
