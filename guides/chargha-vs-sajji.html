Lahori Chargha vs Sajji: What Is the Difference?

Quick answer: Chargha is typically deeply seasoned and cooked for a richly spiced exterior, while sajji is associated with simpler seasoning and a roast-style preparation; recipes vary by region and cook.

The seasoning philosophy is different

Lahori chargha commonly uses an assertive spice marinade that flavours the chicken deeply and creates a bold exterior. Sajji is associated with a simpler seasoning profile that lets the flavour of the roasted meat remain prominent, though modern recipes vary.

Chargha often uses more than one cooking stage

Home chargha methods may steam, roast or otherwise cook the marinated chicken before a final fry or high-heat finish. The exact sequence depends on the recipe, but the goal is tender meat with a strongly seasoned exterior.

Sajji is closely associated with roasting

Sajji has roots in Balochi cooking and is strongly associated with whole or large pieces of meat cooked by roasting. Home oven versions adapt that approach to ordinary kitchen equipment.

The finished textures can differ

Chargha often aims for a richly coloured, spice-coated exterior. Sajji focuses more on juicy roasted meat and a simpler surface seasoning, although restaurant interpretations can blur the distinction.

Do not treat the names as interchangeable

A whole spiced chicken is not automatically chargha or sajji. Marinade style, cooking method and regional tradition all contribute to the identity of the dish.

Which one should you cook?

Choose chargha when you want a bold Lahori-style spice profile and a more elaborate finish. Choose sajji when you want a roast-centred preparation with comparatively restrained seasoning.
