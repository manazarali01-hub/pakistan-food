How to Use Fried Onions in Biryani

Quick answer: For Pakistan Food's six-serving Chicken Biryani, 3 large thinly sliced onions are fried until evenly deep golden; about half are reserved for layering while the rest remain in or return to the chicken masala. Remove them before they turn black because they continue darkening after leaving the oil.

Even slicing is more important than speed

Very thin pieces burn while thick pieces stay pale. Slice across the onions at a consistent thickness and separate the strands before frying. Work in enough oil and avoid overloading a small pan, which traps steam and slows browning.

Deep golden is the target

Fried onion should smell sweet and toasted. Black edges taste bitter and can affect an entire biryani after dum. As soon as the batch reaches deep golden, lift it out and spread it on a plate or rack so carryover heat does not keep cooking a dense pile.

Use onions in two different ways

  • In the masala: fried onion gives body and savoury sweetness to the chicken base.
  • Between/on top of layers: reserved onion gives aromatic bursts and some texture.

Do not pour a bowl of oil-soaked onions into the rice. Drain them briefly; the biryani already contains cooking fat in its masala.

When to crush them

For biryani, the onions used in layers can stay as strands. If some are going into a sauce-like masala, light crushing can help them disperse. Do this only after they cool enough to become crisp; hot soft onions turn into an oily paste.

Protect the reserved portion from steam

Keep the layering onions uncovered until assembly. If you seal them in a container while hot, condensation makes them soft before they ever reach the biryani.
