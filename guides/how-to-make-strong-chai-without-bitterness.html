How to Make Strong Chai Without Bitterness

Quick answer: Increase strength mainly with a measured amount of tea leaves, not an extra-long boil. For two cups of masala chai, Pakistan Food starts with about 2 tsp black tea in 1 cup water plus 1½ cups milk. Brew spices first, add tea later, and keep leaf extraction brief.

Strength and bitterness come from different choices

Strong chai has clear tea flavour that still tastes rounded with milk. Bitter chai feels drying or rough on the tongue. Black tea releases desirable colour and aroma quickly, but longer boiling continues extracting tannins. That is why ten minutes of boiling is not simply “twice as strong” as five minutes.

Build spice aroma before the tea leaves

For masala chai, simmer ginger, green cardamom and cinnamon in water first. Once the spices are fragrant, add the black tea. This gives the spices time without forcing the tea leaves through the same long extraction.

Adjust one measurable thing next batch

If you want...Try this
More tea flavourAdd a small amount more leaf, such as ¼–½ tsp for a two-cup batch.
Less bitternessShorten the leaf simmer before milk.
More bodyUse slightly more milk rather than more tea.
More spiceIncrease ginger/cardamom separately from the tea.

CTC tea can be especially fast

Small CTC granules expose a lot of surface area and can become strong quickly. If your regular brand is powerful, start below the recipe amount and learn its behaviour. Whole-leaf teas may extract differently.

If the chai is already bitter

Strain the leaves immediately. A small amount of hot milk or hot water can soften concentration, but it cannot fully remove heavy over-extraction. Recheck sugar only after the final liquid ratio is settled; sweetness can hide bitterness but also make the cup cloying.
