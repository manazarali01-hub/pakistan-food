Why Does Sugar Syrup Crystallize? Pakistani Sweets Guide

Quick answer: Why does chashni crystallize? Learn how undissolved sugar, pan sides and agitation can trigger crystals in sweet syrup.

Sugar was not fully dissolved

Before bringing the syrup to a boil, stir until the sugar crystals dissolve in the water. Undissolved grains left at the bottom can seed crystallization as the syrup cools.

Crystals remain on the pan sides

Sugar crystals on the sides of the pan can fall back into the syrup and start new crystals. While the syrup is heating, carefully wash down the sides with a clean wet pastry brush or cover the pan briefly to let steam dissolve them.

The syrup is agitated at the wrong stage

Once the sugar has dissolved and the syrup is boiling, avoid unnecessary stirring if the recipe calls for a clear syrup. Swirl the pan gently instead of scraping crystals from the sides into the liquid.

Concentration becomes too high

Boiling too long removes too much water and makes the syrup more concentrated, which can encourage crystals. Follow the recipe's consistency cue and remove the pan from heat when it reaches that stage.

A dirty utensil can seed crystals

Use a clean spoon and pan without sugar grains or crumbs. Even a few crystals can act as a starting point for more crystals as the syrup cools.

Control cooling and storage

Pour the syrup into a clean container and avoid scraping dried crystals from the pan sides into it. Let it cool as directed and keep the rim clean when storing leftovers.
