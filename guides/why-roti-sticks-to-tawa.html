Why Does Roti Stick to the Tawa?

Quick answer: A wet surface, unsuitable tawa temperature or damaged cookware can cause sticking.

Tawa temperature is too low

This is the first thing to check because it directly changes how the dough behaves on the board or tawa.

Dough surface is too wet

Use a gentle, consistent technique rather than compensating with extra flour, fat or cooking time.

Excess loose flour can scorch and stick

Watch the surface and feel the dough. Those cues tell you more than a fixed time when flour hydration varies.

A damaged pan surface causes trouble

Make small corrections while the dough is still workable; large changes late in the process are harder to balance.

Wait for the first side to set

Heat control matters most once the bread reaches the tawa. Adjust between batches as the pan becomes hotter.

Keep the tawa clean between rotis

Keep the successful dough feel, rolling pressure and tawa heat consistent so the next batch behaves the same way.
