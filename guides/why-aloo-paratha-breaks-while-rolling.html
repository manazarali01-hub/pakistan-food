Why Does Aloo Paratha Break While Rolling?

Quick answer: Stuffed aloo paratha usually tears when the dough is too dry or tight, the potato filling is wet or lumpy, too much filling is packed inside, or heavy rolling pressure pushes the filling through weak spots. Use rested pliable dough, a cool fairly dry filling and gentle even rolling.

Dry or tight dough tears more easily

Stuffed paratha dough needs enough flexibility to stretch around the filling. If it feels stiff, cracked or dry before stuffing, rolling can expose those weak points. Add water gradually when kneading and give the dough time to rest so it becomes easier to handle.

Wet potato filling weakens the dough

Potatoes should be cooked until tender but the filling should not contain unnecessary free moisture. Watery chutneys, very wet onions or other additions can soften the inner surface of the dough. Keep the filling cohesive and let hot potatoes cool before stuffing.

Lumps can puncture the paratha

Large pieces of potato or hard spice fragments create pressure points under the rolling pin. Mash the potatoes evenly and distribute seasonings well. A smooth filling spreads more predictably as the dough expands.

Overfilling makes sealing and rolling harder

A generous filling is appealing, but the dough still needs enough room to close securely around it. If the stuffed ball feels stretched before rolling begins, reduce the filling slightly. A balanced dough-to-filling amount is easier to flatten without bursting.

Seal the stuffed dough without trapping weak folds

Bring the edges together evenly and close the seam before flattening. Very thick folds at one point and paper-thin dough at another produce uneven resistance. Press the sealed ball gently with your hands first so the filling begins spreading before the rolling pin does the work.

Roll gently from the centre outward

Heavy pressure can force filling toward the edge faster than the dough can stretch. Dust only as much flour as needed to prevent sticking, rotate the paratha and use light, even strokes. If a tiny tear appears, stop forcing it and patch gently rather than continuing to roll aggressively.
