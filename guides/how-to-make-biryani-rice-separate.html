How to Keep Biryani Rice Separate and Fluffy

Quick answer: Separate biryani grains come from rinsing well, controlled soaking, plenty of boiling water and stopping the rice before it is fully cooked.

Rinse away loose surface starch

Wash basmati gently in several changes of water until the water becomes much clearer. Excess loose starch encourages grains to cling together during boiling.

Soak with control

Soaking hydrates long-grain rice so it cooks more evenly, but very long soaking can make grains fragile. Follow the timing appropriate to your rice rather than assuming longer is always better.

Use plenty of boiling water for parboiling

Biryani rice is commonly boiled in abundant salted water before layering. Enough water lets the grains move freely and makes it easier to drain them at the correct stage.

Drain before the rice is fully cooked

The grain should still have enough firmness to survive layering and the final dum. If it is already completely soft before assembly, steam from the masala can push it toward mushiness.

Handle the rice gently after draining

Avoid aggressive stirring, compacting or repeated mixing. Layer with a light hand so long grains remain intact and steam can move through the pot.

Control moisture during dum

A very wet masala can over-steam the rice even when parboiling was correct. Reduce the masala to the intended consistency and seal the pot so the final cooking stage is controlled rather than watery.

Urdu / Roman Urdu: Biryani ke chawal alag alag kaise rakhein?

Roman Urdu: Rice ko achi tarah rinse karein, soaking aur parboiling ko control karein aur grains ko poora soft hone se pehle layering ke liye nikal lein. Dum ke duran extra moisture aur unnecessary stirring se bachna bhi zaroori hai.
