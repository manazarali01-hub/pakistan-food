How to Use Cardamom in Chai

Quick answer: For two cups of Pakistani milk tea, start with 1–2 green cardamom pods, lightly crushed just before brewing. Add them early enough to perfume the liquid, but do not keep boiling the chai only to extract more cardamom—the floral aroma becomes flatter while the tea itself can turn harsh.

Green cardamom is the usual chai choice

Green cardamom has a sweet, floral, citrus-like aroma that fits doodh patti and masala chai. Black cardamom is much larger and smokier. It can work in savoury masala blends, but it is not a one-for-one replacement for green cardamom in everyday milk tea; even a small amount can make chai taste smoky and medicinal.

How much cardamom per cup?

Chai quantityStarting amountResult
1 cup1 small green podNoticeable but gentle aroma
2 cups1–2 green podsBalanced everyday chai
4 cups3–4 green podsClear cardamom aroma without dominating

Pod size and freshness vary, so treat these as starting points. Freshly opened pods can be far stronger than cardamom that has sat ground in a jar for months.

Crush it lightly, not into powder

Press each pod once with the flat side of a knife or a mortar until the shell splits and the seeds are exposed. This releases aroma while keeping the seeds easy to strain. Grinding the seeds into fine powder gives faster extraction but can leave sediment and can make the spice feel stronger than expected.

When should cardamom go into chai?

For doodh patti or milk tea, add lightly crushed pods while the liquid is heating so they get a few minutes of contact with water and milk. If you are making a stronger masala chai, cardamom can go in with other whole spices before the tea leaves. The important point is not to extend the final tea boil only for cardamom: black tea continues extracting tannins while you wait.

How cardamom interacts with tea, milk and ginger

  • Strong tea: a very strong black-tea brew can hide a single pod, but adding many pods is not the only answer. Shorten harsh tea extraction first.
  • Milk: milk softens sharp spice edges and carries cardamom aroma well, so milk-heavy doodh patti often needs less spice than water-heavy masala chai.
  • Ginger: fresh ginger is assertive and hot. If you use a generous piece of ginger, keep cardamom modest so the cup does not taste crowded.
  • Cinnamon/clove: both are stronger and darker than cardamom. Use them sparingly if the goal is still a cardamom-led chai.
  • Sugar: sweetness can make cardamom seem rounder, but sugar should not be used to hide burnt tea or excessive spice.

Common cardamom mistakes

  • Using stale pre-ground cardamom and compensating with a large quantity.
  • Replacing green cardamom with black cardamom at the same quantity.
  • Crushing pods hours in advance and losing volatile aroma before brewing.
  • Boiling chai much longer just to make the cardamom stronger, which can make the tea bitter.
  • Combining cardamom, clove, cinnamon and ginger at full strength so no single flavour remains clear.

Troubleshooting

Cardamom flavour is too weak

Use fresher pods, crush them immediately before brewing, or increase by half to one pod for the next two-cup batch. Do not automatically lengthen tea-leaf boiling time.

Chai tastes perfumed or medicinal

Use fewer pods next time and check whether another strong spice is contributing. If the cup is already made, extra milk can soften the intensity, but it will also change tea strength and sweetness.

There are gritty specks in the cup

Use lightly crushed whole pods rather than finely ground cardamom and strain the chai before serving.
