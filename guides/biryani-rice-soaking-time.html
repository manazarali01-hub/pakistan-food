Biryani Rice Soaking Time: Choose by Rice Type

Quick answer: For many raw-basmati biryani recipes, 20–30 minutes is a useful starting point. Rice age, grain thickness and processing affect hydration, so follow the packet or trusted recipe—especially for sella rice—and avoid assuming one timing fits every variety.

Choose a starting point for your rice

Raw basmati, sella/parboiled basmati and other long-grain rice may respond differently to soaking. Follow the packet or trusted recipe for the variety you actually bought; do not assume all rice needs the same number of minutes.

Rice typePractical approach
Raw basmatiUse packet or recipe timing as a first test; check the grains during boiling.
Sella / parboiled basmatiFollow its own instructions rather than copying raw-basmati timing.
Other long-grain riceTest a small batch; absorption and cooking behaviour can vary by variety.

Rinse before soaking

Wash the rice gently until the water becomes much clearer. Rinsing removes loose surface starch, while soaking then hydrates the grain more evenly before parboiling.

Test the grain instead of trusting the clock alone

Soaking hydrates rice, but the more useful cooking check comes during parboiling. Test a few grains regularly and stop at the firmness required by your biryani method; the rice will continue cooking during dum.

If grains break or turn mushy

Long soaking can be one factor, but rice may also break from over-boiling, rough stirring, squeezing during draining or compressing during layering. Next time, adjust soaking or boiling—not both at once—so you can identify what helped.

If the rice stays hard after dum

Hard grains do not automatically mean the soak was too short. The rice may need more pre-cooking, the dum may be too brief, or moisture may be insufficient. Review the whole process before increasing soaking time alone.

A repeatable small-batch test

  1. Record the rice brand and variety.
  2. Follow the packet or recipe soaking guidance.
  3. Check grains during boiling instead of relying only on a timer.
  4. Drain at the correct stage and handle gently.
  5. After dum, note whether the grains were hard, broken or too soft. Change one variable next time.
Tip: Keep the brand and cooking method steady while testing. Changing rice, soaking, boiling and dum together makes it difficult to identify the cause.

Common questions

Does sella rice need the same soaking time as raw basmati?

Not necessarily. Sella rice is parboiled during processing and brands vary, so use the packet directions or a trusted recipe written for that rice rather than copying raw-basmati timing.

Can I soak biryani rice overnight?

For most biryani methods, an overnight soak is unnecessary and may leave the grains overly hydrated and fragile. Follow the rice package or recipe. If your preparation requires a longer soak, keep the rice refrigerated rather than leaving it at room temperature for hours, then drain it well.

How do I know when rice is ready for dum?

Check a few grains during parboiling. They should reach the firmness your recipe calls for because they will continue cooking under dum. A timer is a starting point, not a substitute for testing the grain.
