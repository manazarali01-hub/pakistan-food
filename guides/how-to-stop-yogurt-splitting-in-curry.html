How to Stop Yogurt from Splitting in Pakistani Curry

Quick answer: Use room-temperature yogurt, whisk it smooth, lower the heat before adding it, and stir steadily until it is incorporated into the masala.

Start with smooth yogurt

Whisk the yogurt before it reaches the pan so there are no cold lumps. Full-fat yogurt is generally more forgiving under heat than very low-fat yogurt because its higher fat and solids help the sauce feel smoother.

Reduce the temperature difference

Very cold yogurt hitting extremely hot masala is more likely to separate visibly. Let the yogurt lose its refrigerator chill and lower the pan heat before incorporating it.

Add gradually and stir steadily

Rather than dropping one large cold mass into the pan, add yogurt in stages when the recipe allows and stir continuously at first. This distributes heat more evenly through the sauce.

Do not rush back to a hard boil

Once the yogurt is incorporated, bring the curry back toward its cooking temperature gradually. Aggressive boiling immediately after addition can make separation more obvious.

Cook the masala until it looks cohesive

Continue according to the recipe until the yogurt is integrated with the onion and spice base and the masala looks glossy. A little fat separating from a properly cooked masala is different from curdled yogurt solids.

If the curry has already split

Lower the heat and stir or whisk gently; for some gravies, careful blending can improve appearance. The flavour may still be fine even if the sauce is not perfectly smooth, so avoid repeatedly adding more dairy in an attempt to hide it.
