How to Thicken Karahi Without Flour

Quick answer: The best way to thicken Pakistani karahi is usually reduction: cook uncovered so excess water evaporates and the tomato masala concentrates around the meat.

Reduction is the natural first choice

Karahi gravy gets body as water evaporates and tomato, spices and cooking fat concentrate around the meat. If the meat is already cooked, uncovering the pan is usually more appropriate than adding a starch thickener.

Use a wide cooking surface

A karahi or broad pan exposes more liquid to the air than a narrow deep pot. The larger surface lets steam escape faster and gives you better control over the final masala consistency.

Increase heat carefully near the end

Once the meat is tender, a moderately stronger heat can speed reduction. Stir and scrape frequently because concentrated tomato masala can catch on the base much faster than a watery gravy.

Do not keep adding water during bhunai

Add liquid when the meat genuinely needs it for cooking, but repeated unnecessary additions make the final reduction longer. Use small amounts and judge the moisture already released by meat and tomatoes.

Avoid flour or cornflour unless the recipe specifically needs it

Starch can thicken liquid quickly but changes the fresh reduced texture associated with karahi. Proper tomato reduction normally gives enough body without making the gravy pasty.

If the karahi is still watery

First confirm the meat is fully cooked, then remove the lid and reduce while stirring. If vegetables such as peppers are already at their ideal texture, avoid overcooking them simply to evaporate sauce.
