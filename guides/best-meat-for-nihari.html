Best Meat for Nihari: Cuts That Stay Tender in Slow Cooking

Quick answer: Nihari benefits from beef or mutton cuts with connective tissue that soften during slow cooking. Shank is a classic choice because long cooking develops a rich broth and tender meat.

Shank is well suited to long cooking

Beef shank is a classic choice because it contains connective tissue that softens during prolonged moist cooking. The same slow process that tenderises the meat also contributes gelatin and body to the broth.

Bone-in and boneless both have advantages

Bone-in pieces can contribute additional richness and traditional presentation, while boneless pieces are easier to portion and serve. A mixture can provide both convenience and depth.

Avoid relying only on very lean quick-cooking cuts

Lean tender cuts are valuable for fast cooking, but nihari needs enough time for its gravy and spices to develop. Cuts with suitable connective tissue tolerate that long process better.

Piece size changes the cooking time

Large shank pieces take longer to become tender than small boneless cubes. Cut consistently so different pieces approach doneness together rather than leaving some dry while others remain tough.

Pressure cooking changes time, not the tenderness target

A pressure cooker can shorten the tenderising stage considerably. Regardless of equipment, judge the meat by whether it yields easily rather than following one universal number of minutes.

Choose the cut for the finished bowl

For a rich traditional-style nihari, prioritise shank or another slow-cooking cut with connective tissue. Then use gentle finishing heat so tender pieces stay intact while the gravy reaches its final consistency.
