Why Is Chai Too Strong?

Quick answer: For about two cups of masala chai, 2 tsp black tea leaves is already a useful starting point. If the cup tastes harsh rather than pleasantly strong, the leaves were probably boiled too long, too much tea was used, or there is too little milk/water for the amount of tea and spices.

Strong tea and bitter tea are not the same

A strong cup has concentrated tea flavour but can still taste rounded with milk and sugar. A bitter or drying cup has extracted more tannin than you want. Extending the boil is a poor way to chase strength because bitterness rises faster than pleasant aroma after a point.

Check leaf quantity first

The Pakistan Food masala chai recipe uses 2 tsp black tea for 1 cup water plus 1½ cups milk. If your tea is a particularly strong CTC blend, even that amount may feel intense. Adjust by small fractions of a teaspoon next time instead of changing several variables at once.

Keep the tea-leaf simmer short

Boil spices in water first so ginger, cardamom and cinnamon can release aroma. Add black tea later and simmer it briefly. Milk can then be added and brought back to a gentle boil. This separates spice extraction from tea extraction and reduces the temptation to boil the leaves for many minutes.

How to fix a cup that is already too strong

  • Too concentrated but not bitter: add a little hot milk or hot water, then recheck sweetness.
  • Bitter/drying: dilution helps somewhat, but do not keep boiling the same leaves.
  • Too spicy: extra milk can soften ginger/cinnamon intensity, but the next batch should use less spice.
  • Too sweet after dilution: do not add more sugar until the final liquid ratio is settled.

Common causes

SymptomLikely cause
Dark and dry on the tongueTea leaves simmered too long
Strong but flatToo much leaf for the liquid, not necessarily enough aroma
Sharp ginger burnSpice load is high even if tea quantity is correct
Milky yet still harshOver-extracted tea cannot always be hidden by more milk
