How to Balance a Curry That Tastes Too Sour

Quick answer: Curry too sour? Learn how to rebalance tomato, yogurt or lemon acidity without simply covering it with sugar.

Identify the source of acidity

Taste a small spoonful first and identify the likely source: tomatoes, yogurt, tamarind, lemon or vinegar. Different acids need different fixes, so do not add sugar before checking the base.

Increase the non-acidic curry base

Increase the non-acidic part of the curry, such as cooked onion masala, meat, vegetables or lentils that suit the recipe. This lowers the proportion of sour ingredients without making the gravy watery.

Add dairy only when the dish suits it

If the curry suits dairy, stir in a little yogurt or cream off the heat, then warm gently. Add yogurt gradually to reduce the risk of splitting; dairy is not suitable for every curry.

Balance concentration before adding sweetness

If the gravy is very concentrated, loosen it with a small splash of unsalted stock or water, then simmer briefly and taste again. Avoid a large pour that thins the curry before the acidity is balanced.

Avoid adding more tomato or lemon

Pause the lemon, tamarind, vinegar or extra tomato until the taste is corrected. Add acidic ingredients in small measured amounts at the end, because simmering can concentrate their flavour.

Recheck salt after correcting acidity

After changing the base, taste again before adjusting salt. A small pinch may round the flavour, but salt does not remove acidity; avoid using sugar as an automatic fix.
