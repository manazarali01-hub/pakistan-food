How to Make Soft Aloo Paratha

Quick answer: Use a soft, rested dough and a dry, lump-free potato filling; roll gently and cook on medium heat so the paratha cooks through without becoming hard.

Make a flexible, rested dough

Add water gradually and knead until the dough is smooth, soft and pliable without being sticky. A short rest allows the flour to hydrate and the gluten to relax, which makes the dough easier to roll without springing back.

Keep the potato filling dry and smooth

Boil potatoes until tender, drain them well and mash out large lumps. Cool the filling before stuffing. Wet filling weakens the dough and large potato pieces can puncture it while rolling.

Seal without trapping excess air

Use enough dough to contain the filling comfortably. Bring the edges together, pinch the seal closed and flatten gently before rolling. If filling squeezes out, use slightly less filling next time rather than adding lots of dry flour.

Roll with light, even pressure

Turn the paratha as you roll so the filling spreads toward the edges. Heavy pressure in one spot creates thin patches that tear while the centre stays thick.

Cook at moderate heat

A cool tawa can dry the bread before it browns, while excessive heat can burn the surface before the inner dough cooks. Use medium to medium-high heat, flip as needed, and add ghee or oil after the surface begins to set.

Why aloo paratha turns hard

Common causes include a stiff dough, too much dry flour during rolling, prolonged cooking on low heat, or leaving cooked parathas uncovered. Stack finished parathas and keep them covered briefly if you want them soft.
