Why Does Naan Get Hard After Cooking?

Quick answer: Dry dough, overcooking and uncovered cooling can make naan lose softness.

Dry dough loses softness quickly

Check this factor first because it has a direct effect on texture or flavour and is easier to correct early.

Too much flour during shaping

Use controlled heat or handling so the ingredient develops without drying, scorching or becoming diluted.

Overcooking drives out moisture

Look for the physical cue described here rather than relying only on a fixed number of minutes.

Low heat can prolong drying

Avoid adding several corrective ingredients at once; that makes the original imbalance harder to diagnose.

Uncovered naan loses steam

As moisture changes, the same heat behaves differently. Reduce or increase it deliberately as the dish progresses.

Hold cooked naan covered

Once the desired stage is reached, move on instead of continuing automatically. This protects the balance you have built.
