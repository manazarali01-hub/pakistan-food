How to Cook Paya Until Tender

Quick answer: Paya needs slow, moist cooking until connective tissue softens and the broth becomes naturally rich; a pressure cooker can shorten the process.

Why paya needs patient cooking

Trotters contain a large amount of collagen-rich connective tissue. Moist heat gradually converts collagen into gelatin, which is why properly cooked paya develops both tenderness and a broth with natural body.

Clean and prepare before cooking

Use properly cleaned trotters from a reliable butcher and rinse them as appropriate for your recipe. Build the spice base separately or with the meat according to the method you are following; good preparation is easier than trying to correct unwanted flavours at the end.

Pressure cooker or heavy pot?

A pressure cooker greatly shortens the tenderising stage, while a heavy covered pot gives more visual control but needs much longer. Exact time varies with the animal, size of the pieces and cooker, so tenderness matters more than a rigid clock time.

How to tell when paya is ready

The connective tissue around the bones should be very soft rather than rubbery, and the broth should feel richer after simmering. If the meat and tissue still resist easily, continue moist cooking instead of simply reducing the gravy.

Finish the broth separately from tenderising

Once the paya is tender, simmer uncovered if the broth is too thin. This is also the best stage to make final salt and spice adjustments because reduction concentrates seasoning.

Common mistake: reducing too early

Do not let the cooking liquid run dry while the trotters are still tough. Tenderise first with adequate liquid; concentrate the broth only after the texture is where you want it.
