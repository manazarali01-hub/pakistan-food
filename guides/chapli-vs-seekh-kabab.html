Chapli Kabab vs Seekh Kabab: What Is the Difference?

Quick answer: Chapli kabab is a flat, pan-fried minced-meat patty with a coarse seasoned texture; seekh kabab is shaped around skewers and cooked over direct heat or a grill.

Shape changes the cooking behaviour

Chapli kabab is broad and flat, giving it a large surface for pan browning. Seekh kabab is formed into a long cylinder around a skewer, so the mixture must cling securely while cooking.

The mixtures have different texture goals

Chapli kabab often keeps visible herbs, spices and textured additions in the mince. Seekh mixture is generally worked into a more cohesive mass so it can be shaped firmly around the skewer.

Cooking methods are different

Chapli kabab is commonly shallow-fried on a pan or griddle. Seekh kabab is associated with direct heat on skewers, although home cooks can adapt it to a grill, oven or suitable pan.

Moisture control matters for both

Excess liquid can make chapli patties fragile and seekh mixture slide from the skewer. Drain very wet additions and follow the recipe's binder and chilling guidance rather than adding large amounts of dry ingredients.

Do not overcook for colour

Both styles benefit from good browning, but extended cooking dries minced meat. Use adequate heat to colour the exterior while cooking the centre safely through.

Which kabab should you choose?

Choose chapli for a rustic, flat patty with a coarser bite and pan-fried crust. Choose seekh for a cohesive grilled kabab with smoky potential and the characteristic skewer shape.
