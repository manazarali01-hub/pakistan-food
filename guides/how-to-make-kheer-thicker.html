How to Make Rice Kheer Thicker and Creamier

Quick answer: Thicker kheer comes from slow reduction and properly cooked rice. Simmer gently, stir regularly and allow the milk to reduce instead of adding large amounts of starch.

Let the rice provide natural body

Cook the rice until very tender so some starch disperses into the milk. If the grains remain firm, the kheer can look thin even after the milk has reduced considerably.

Use gentle reduction instead of extreme heat

A steady simmer evaporates water and concentrates milk solids. Very high heat increases the risk of scorching the base and sides before the dessert reaches an even creamy consistency.

Stir and scrape the pan regularly

As kheer thickens, milk solids settle more readily on hot surfaces. Scrape the bottom and sides frequently and stir the thickened milk back into the dessert before it catches.

Do not judge thickness only while boiling

Hot kheer is looser than chilled kheer. Stop cooking when it is slightly thinner than the final cold texture you want, especially if it will be refrigerated before serving.

If the kheer is still too thin

Continue gentle uncovered reduction rather than immediately adding a large quantity of starch. If the rice itself is undercooked, allow it to soften first so natural starch can contribute.

If the kheer becomes too thick

Loosen it gradually with milk, preferably while warming gently, until the desired consistency returns. Add small amounts because a little liquid can make a noticeable difference.
