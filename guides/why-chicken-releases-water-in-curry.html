Why Does Chicken Release Water in Curry?

Quick answer: Why does chicken release water in curry and karahi? Learn how meat moisture, crowding and heat affect the masala.

Chicken naturally contains moisture

Chicken naturally releases moisture, especially boneless pieces. Thaw it fully in the refrigerator if frozen, drain well and pat the surface dry before it goes into the pan.

A crowded pan traps released water

Leave space between pieces so steam can escape. Brown chicken in batches if the pan is crowded; a full pan often stews the meat in released liquid instead of cooking the masala down.

Low heat slows evaporation

Use enough heat to evaporate the liquid once the chicken releases it, but do not scorch the onion or spice base. Let excess water cook off before adding more liquid ingredients.

Wet marinade adds extra liquid

Shake or scrape off excess watery marinade before cooking. Thick yogurt and a measured marinade coat the chicken better than a large pool of thin liquid.

Frozen or poorly drained chicken contributes water

Frozen chicken or water left after rinsing can cool the pan and add extra liquid. Thaw and drain safely first, then cook in batches rather than adding frozen pieces to hot oil.

Reduce moisture before concentrating masala

Once the chicken is cooked through, simmer the masala uncovered to reduce excess liquid. If the chicken is already tender, lift it out briefly while the gravy reduces, then return it to coat without overcooking.

Roman Urdu: Chicken salan mein pani kyun chhorta hai?

Roman Urdu: Chicken naturally moisture release karta hai; crowded pan aur low heat par yeh pani zyada visible reh sakta hai. Heat aur reduction ko control karein.
