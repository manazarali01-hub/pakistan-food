Why Does Curry Get Too Oily? 6 Balance Mistakes

Quick answer: Why does Pakistani curry become too oily? Learn how excess fat, weak emulsification and cooking balance affect the finished masala.

Too much fat was added at the start

Measure oil or ghee rather than pouring freely. Some Pakistani curries naturally release a little fat when the masala is cooked well, but a deep pool of oil can mean the starting amount was too high.

The curry base is too small for the oil

If there is far more oil than curry base, add a little more cooked onion-tomato masala or another unsalted base that suits the recipe. Let it simmer and taste before adding another portion.

Masala has not emulsified with the base

Simmer gently so the masala and liquid settle together. Stir carefully around meat or vegetables; vigorous stirring will not remove excess fat and may break delicate pieces.

Water reduced too far

If the gravy has reduced until oil pools on top, add a small splash of hot water or stock only if the recipe allows it, then simmer until the consistency returns. Avoid flooding the pot.

Fat-rich ingredients changed the balance

Cream, coconut milk, nut paste and fatty meat can all add richness. Count them as part of the fat balance and reduce the extra oil or ghee when the recipe already includes several rich ingredients.

Fix the ratio instead of hiding the oil

Skim visible excess oil with a spoon after the curry settles. For suitable dishes, chilling leftovers can solidify surface fat for easier removal; do not add flour or raw potato just to hide the oil.
