Why Does Curry Get Too Oily? 6 Balance Mistakes

Quick answer: Why does Pakistani curry become too oily? Learn how excess fat, weak emulsification and cooking balance affect the finished masala.

Too much fat was added at the start

This is a primary control point for the result. Check it before trying to compensate later in cooking, because early texture and moisture decisions carry through to the finished dish.

The curry base is too small for the oil

Use moderate, responsive heat and observe the food rather than relying on time alone. The correct stage should look and smell ready before you continue.

Masala has not emulsified with the base

Handle the food gently once its structure has softened. Extra stirring, pressing or prolonged cooking can turn a small issue into a larger texture problem.

Water reduced too far

Make one correction at a time. Large last-minute additions often solve one imbalance while creating another in salt, moisture, fat or texture.

Fat-rich ingredients changed the balance

Cookware, ingredient moisture and stove strength vary, so use visible cues alongside measurements. This makes the method repeatable in a home kitchen.

Fix the ratio instead of hiding the oil

For the next batch, change this variable first and keep the others consistent. That is the simplest way to identify the real cause and improve the result.
