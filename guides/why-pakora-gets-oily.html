Why Pakora Gets Oily and How to Fix It

Quick answer: Pakora often becomes oily when frying oil is not hot enough, the batter is too thin, or too many pieces are added at once and drop the oil temperature.

Oil temperature is usually the first thing to check

When frying oil is not hot enough, the batter can absorb more fat before its outer structure sets. Adding a large batch at once also drops the oil temperature suddenly, even if the oil seemed hot beforehand.

Keep the batter thick enough to cling

Pakora batter should coat the ingredients instead of flowing off like thin liquid. Add water gradually because onions and other vegetables release additional moisture after salt and spices are mixed in.

Do not overcrowd the pan

Leave space around each piece so hot oil can circulate. Smaller batches recover temperature faster and usually give more consistent browning than filling the pan all at once.

Adjust heat during frying

One fixed burner setting may not work for the whole batch. The temperature falls when food enters and rises again as moisture escapes, so make small heat adjustments rather than letting the oil smoke or cool excessively.

Drain without trapping steam

Lift cooked pakoras onto a rack or absorbent surface and avoid covering them while they are very hot. Trapped steam softens the crust and can make the surface feel heavier or greasier.

If pakoras are crisp outside but raw inside

The oil may be too hot or the pieces too large. Reduce the heat slightly and make smaller, more even portions so the centre cooks before the exterior becomes too dark.
