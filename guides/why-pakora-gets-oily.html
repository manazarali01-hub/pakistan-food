Why Pakora Gets Oily and How to Fix It

Quick answer: Pakora often becomes oily when frying oil is not hot enough, the batter is too thin, or too many pieces are added at once and drop the oil temperature.

Check batter consistency

The batter should coat the ingredients rather than run off like water. Add liquid gradually because vegetables can release moisture.

Keep oil temperature steady

Oil that cools too much encourages batter to absorb fat before a crust forms. Fry in manageable batches.

Drain without trapping steam

Lift pakoras onto a rack or absorbent surface and avoid covering them while hot. Trapped steam softens the crust.
